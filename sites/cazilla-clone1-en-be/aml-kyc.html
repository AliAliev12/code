A plain-language guide to identity checks, anti-money laundering rules, and source of funds requirements that apply to players based in Belgium.

This page explains the Anti-Money Laundering (AML) and Know Your Customer (KYC) rules that shape how a top online casino like Cazilla handles player verification in Belgium. These are not internal policies a casino can choose to ignore — they follow directly from Belgian gambling legislation and EU directives aimed at preventing financial crime and keeping players safe. Everything here is provided for transparency and does not amount to legal advice.

Why identity verification is required

Every licensed operator has a legal duty to confirm who its players actually are before handling significant transactions. When you sign up and start playing best online casino games, the platform must collect and verify personal details: your full name, date of birth, home address, and a valid government-issued document such as a national identity card or passport. The point is straightforward — to make sure accounts are not opened by minors, by people under self-exclusion orders, or by anyone trying to exploit gambling services for illegal ends.

Verification usually happens during registration or when you submit a withdrawal request. Certain account activity patterns can also trigger additional checks. If you delay sending the required documents, withdrawals may be put on hold until the review wraps up.

Source of funds and enhanced due diligence

Identity checks are just the starting point. Belgian AML rules also require operators to look at where the money used for gambling actually comes from. If your deposits or betting volumes cross certain thresholds, the casino may ask for supporting documents — recent payslips, bank statements, or other proof of legitimate income. This applies to all account holders regardless of nationality; it is standard practice across the regulated market, not something targeted at specific players.

People classified as politically exposed persons (PEPs), or those with connections to higher-risk countries, may face additional scrutiny. These enhanced checks are a legal requirement, not a sign that anything suspicious has been flagged against you personally. Our reviews online casino coverage regularly finds that operators who take due diligence seriously tend to be more reliable and financially stable — which matters for anyone playing with real money.

How AML obligations protect you as a player

A well-run AML programme is not just a regulatory box-ticking exercise — it genuinely benefits players. When an operator consistently screens transactions and verifies identities, it becomes a much harder target for fraud and money laundering. Those activities can seriously damage a casino's financial health and, in turn, hurt the legitimate customers who rely on it. It is one of the main reasons independent editorial sites like this one treat licensing and regulatory compliance as a core part of any casino evaluation.
