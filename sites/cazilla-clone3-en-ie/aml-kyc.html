Why AML and KYC Obligations Exist

Licensed gambling operators are legally required to verify every customer's identity and to keep an eye on transactions for anything that looks like financial crime. These rules come from the relevant licensing authority and sit in line with EU Anti-Money Laundering Directives. The purpose is to stop money laundering, fraud, and the funding of illegal activity. Compliance is not a choice — it is a condition of holding a gambling licence, and any casino worth its salt takes that seriously. If you do not complete verification, you can expect withdrawals to be frozen or your account closed altogether.

Identity Checks and Document Requirements

Once you register, and before your first withdrawal goes through, you will need to submit documents that confirm who you are and where you live. Usually that means a government-issued photo ID — a passport or driving licence — along with a recent utility bill or bank statement from the last three months. Some platforms will also ask for a selfie or a photo of you holding your ID. Further checks can kick in if your account activity crosses certain thresholds. At that stage the casino may ask for proof of your source of funds. That might mean recent payslips, a bank statement showing regular income, or paperwork relating to a one-off event like an inheritance or property sale. Getting those documents in quickly keeps things moving and prevents unnecessary delays on your account.

Source of Funds and Enhanced Due Diligence

Source of funds verification is a more detailed layer of AML compliance that applies when a player's deposits or wagers go beyond set limits. The operator needs to be satisfied that the money you are gambling with comes from a legitimate place. This is not about singling anyone out — it is a regulatory requirement, full stop.
